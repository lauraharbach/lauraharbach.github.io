<h2>Background</h2>

<p>My academic journey began with a deep fascination for the complex dynamics of natural systems. I completed my Master of Physics (MPhys) in Astrophysics at the  <strong>University of Southampton</strong>, spending a transformative year as a Visiting Student Researcher at the  <strong>Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics (CfA)</strong> in Cambridge, Boston, USA. There, I used 3D Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations to investigate how stellar winds from active M-dwarf stars impact close-in exoplanet atmospheres.</p>

<p>From there I went on to pursue a PhD in Physics at <strong>Imperial College London</strong>, funded by the STFC. Shifting to hydrodynamics, my doctoral research in the domain of computational astrophysics focused on 1D atmospheric modeling, developing multifluid hydrodynamics models that moved beyond simple hydrogen/helium compositions to investigate the atmospheric evolution and escape mechanisms of water-rich exoplanets.</p>

<p>During my PhD, I became increasingly drawn to applying computational methodologies to pressing real-world challenges. A <strong>DiRAC Innovation Placement</strong> collaborating with <strong>Save the Children International</strong> and computer scientists at <strong>Brunel University of London</strong> ignited my transition into humanitarian computing, using agent-based models (ABMs) to simulate human migration in response to flooding.</p>

<h2>Current Research Interests</h2>

<p> I am currently a Postdoctoral Researcher in the <strong> Modelling and Simulation Group in the Department of Computer Science at Brunel University London</strong>. Broadly speaking, my work involves applying computational skills and techniques to translate complex real-world problems into computational models for simulation and analysis, with an emphasis on understanding how trustworthy the models and their results are.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>
        <strong>Migration & Return Modelling (Ukraine):</strong> Conducting scenario analysis and simulation of Ukrainian returnees from Europe across various war progression trajectories, in collaboration with UNHCR.
        </li>
        <li>
        <strong>Climate & Disaster-Driven Displacement:</strong> Modelling refugee flows, human migration, and population movement triggered by natural disasters, severe flooding, and climate events to support decision-making in humanitarian response.
       </li>
        <li>
        <strong>Sensitivity Analysis in Migration Modelling:</strong> Applying global sensitivity analysis to agent-based forced migration frameworks to evaluate parameter importance, structural assumptions, and prediction uncertainty in scenario forecasting.
       </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Uncertainty Quantification &amp; Exascale HPC Tools:</strong> Engaging with national exascale computing initiatives through the SEAVEA (Software Environment for Actionable & VVUQ-evaluated Exascale Applications) project under the UK's ExCALIBUR (Exascale Computing Algorithms & Infrastructures Benefiting UK Research) programme. Focusing on applying Verification, Validation, and Uncertainty Quantification (VVUQ) methodologies across computational models while supporting the open-source SEAVEA toolkit. For example, applying UQ techniques to AI generative models like REINVENT4 for <em>de novo</em> drug discovery.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Uncertainty Qualification:</strong> Developing, benchmarking, and applying qualitative Uncertainty Qualification frameworks (evaluating non-numerical modelling assumptions, qualitative structural choices, and parameter calibration) across high-performance computing simulations and computational models.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Agent-Based Modelling &amp; Complex Systems:</strong> Designing rule-based computational simulations for social dynamics, forced displacement, and multi-agent interaction.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>IoT Systems &amp; Uncertainty Quantification:</strong> Contributing to the HOMEPOT project (ITEA4), focused on applying UQ methodologies to Internet of Things (IoT) architectures and developing computational solutions for the homogeneous management and orchestration of diverse devices.
        </li>
      </ul>
